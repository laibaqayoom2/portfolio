The Problem

Every section was shouting at once

01

No focal point

The hero crammed a search form, a small property photo, and page copy into one dense strip — nothing told the visitor where to look first.

02

Repetition instead of structure

Listings were split into separate blocks per city (Lahore, Islamabad, Karachi), repeating the same card design three times instead of one unified way to browse.

03

Flat visual weight

Property cards, tool widgets, and text blocks all carried similar size and spacing, so nothing on the page read as more or less important.

The Goals

What the redesign had to do

01

Lead with trust

Rentals are a high-stakes search. The page needed to say "verified" before it said anything else.

02

One system for listings

Replace three parallel city sections with a single, filterable property grid.

03

Keep every feature

Nothing was cut — the size converter, the process steps, news & forums all stay, just reorganized.

Key Decisions

Four changes that carry the page

01

A hero that sells the promise, not the form

The old hero tried to be a search tool first. The new one leads with a full-bleed photo and a single line — "A home to rent, verified before you see it" — with the search card floating on top instead of competing for space beside it.

02

One grid instead of three

Lahore, Islamabad, and Karachi listings merged into a single "Discover our Rental properties" grid with a city/type filter bar above it — same content, one consistent card, one place to scan.

03

Tools get room to breathe

The property-size converter moved from a small inline widget into a full section with its own image, benefit list, and a clear "Explore Now" action — treated as a feature, not an afterthought.

04

Consistent spacing rhythm

Every section now shares the same padding, card radius, and type scale, so the page reads as one product rather than stacked widgets from different templates.

Reflection

What this redesign was really about

A rental marketplace lives or dies on whether people trust the listings enough to visit. The old page buried that trust signal under equal-weight sections. The redesign doesn't add anything new — it just decides, section by section, what deserves the visitor's attention first.
